# Resume Guidelines - Complete Standards and Requirements
## Overview
Comprehensive guidelines for creating professional, effective resumes that meet industry standards and maximize interview opportunities.
## 1. Essential Resume Components
### Required Sections (Must Include)
1. **Contact Information**
2. **Professional Summary or Objective**
3. **Work Experience**
4. **Education**
5. **Skills**
### Optional Sections (Include When Relevant)
6. **Certifications and Licenses**
7. **Projects**
8. **Awards and Honors**
9. **Publications**
10. **Professional Affiliations**
11. **Volunteer Experience**
12. **Languages**
13. **Technical Proficiencies**
14. **Patents**
15. **Speaking Engagements**
### Sections to AVOID
- References (provide separately when requested)
- "References Available Upon Request" (assumed)
- Hobbies (unless directly relevant)
- Personal information (age, marital status, photo in US)
- Salary information
- Reasons for leaving jobs
- Irrelevant work from 15+ years ago
## 2. Contact Information Standards
### Required Elements
Full Legal Name (or preferred professional name)
Phone Number: (555) 123-4567
Email: professional.email@provider.com
Location: City, State (or City, Country if international)
LinkedIn: linkedin.com/in/yourprofile
### Professional Email Guidelines
**Good Examples:**
- firstname.lastname@email.com
- firstnamelastname@email.com
- f.lastname@email.com
- firstname.initial@email.com
**Bad Examples (Avoid):**
- partygirl123@email.com
- cooldude@email.com
- ilovecats@email.com
- Current employer email address
- Outdated providers (aol.com, hotmail.com less professional)
### Phone Number Best Practices
- Use single, reliable number
- Include area code
- Ensure voicemail is professional
- Format consistently: (555) 123-4567 or 555-123-4567
- International: +1-555-123-4567
### Location Information
- City and State sufficient (no full street address)
- Include country if applying internationally
- If relocating, consider: "San Francisco, CA (relocating from New York, NY)"
- Remote workers: "Remote" or "Remote - Available Nationwide"
### Online Presence
**Include:**
- LinkedIn profile (essential)
- Professional portfolio website
- GitHub (for developers)
- Behance/Dribbble (for designers)
- Professional blog (if relevant)
**Exclude:**
- Facebook, Instagram, Twitter (unless professional brand)
- Snapchat, TikTok
- Dating profiles
- Any unprofessional content
## 3. Professional Summary Guidelines
### Length and Placement
- **Length**: 3-5 lines (50-150 words)
- **Placement**: Directly below contact information
- **Format**: Paragraph or 3-4 bullet points
### Content Components
1. **Professional Identity**: Job title and years of experience
2. **Specialization**: Industry, domain, or technical expertise
3. **Key Achievement**: Most impressive quantified result
4. **Core Competencies**: 3-5 relevant skills
5. **Value Proposition**: What you offer to employer
### Writing Formula
[Professional Title] with [X years] experience in [Industry/Domain] |
[Specialization/Expertise] | [Key Achievement with metrics] |
[Core Skills: Skill 1, Skill 2, Skill 3] |
[Career Goal or Value Proposition]
### Examples by Experience Level
**Entry-Level:**
Recent Computer Science graduate from UC Berkeley with internship experience
at leading tech companies. Developed mobile application with 10,000+ downloads
during senior capstone project. Proficient in Python, Java, React, and SQL.
Passionate about creating user-centered software solutions. Seeking software
engineering role to contribute technical skills and innovative thinking.

Strategic Digital Marketing Manager with 6 years of experience driving growth
for B2B SaaS companies. Increased organic traffic by 250% and generated $2M in
pipeline revenue through content marketing and SEO optimization. Expert in
marketing automation, analytics, and cross-functional team leadership. Proven
ability to scale marketing operations and exceed ROI targets.
**Senior-Level:**
Senior Product Manager with 12+ years leading product strategy for enterprise
software solutions. Successfully launched 15+ products generating $50M+ in
annual revenue. Expert in agile methodologies, roadmap development, and
stakeholder management. Known for translating customer needs into innovative
products that achieve 95%+ satisfaction ratings. Seeking CPO role to drive
product vision and organizational growth.
**Executive-Level:**
Transformational Chief Technology Officer with 20 years of experience scaling

across multiple continents, delivering cloud-native platforms serving 10M+ users.
Track record of reducing infrastructure costs by 40% while improving system

## 4. Work Experience Guidelines
### Entry Structure
Job Title (Bold, 12-14pt)
Company Name | City, State
Month Year - Month Year (or Present)
• Achievement-focused bullet point with quantified results
• Responsibility description using action verbs and keywords
• Project or initiative with measurable impact
• Team leadership or collaboration highlight
• Technical skills or tools utilized
### Bullet Point Guidelines
**Number of Bullets per Position:**
- Current role: 5-8 bullets
- Recent roles (last 5 years): 4-6 bullets
- Older roles (5-10 years): 2-4 bullets
- Early career roles (10+ years): 1-2 bullets or omit
**Bullet Point Formula:**
[Action Verb] + [What You Did] + [How You Did It] + [Result/Impact with Metrics]
**Strong Examples:**
• Increased sales revenue by 35% ($2M) in 12 months by implementing new
CRM system and training team of 15 sales representatives on consultative
selling techniques
• Reduced customer churn from 12% to 6% by developing proactive retention
program, resulting in $500K annual recurring revenue preservation
• Led cross-functional team of 8 to launch mobile app ahead of schedule,
achieving 50,000 downloads and 4.8-star rating in first quarter
• Streamlined supply chain operations, cutting procurement costs by $1.2M
annually while improving delivery times by 25%
**Weak Examples (Avoid):**
• Responsible for sales
• Managed team
• Worked on various projects
• Helped with customer service
• Assisted manager with tasks
### Action Verbs by Category
**Leadership:**
- Directed, Led, Managed, Supervised, Coordinated, Orchestrated, Spearheaded
- Mentored, Coached, Trained, Developed, Guided, Cultivated, Empowered
**Achievement:**
- Achieved, Exceeded, Surpassed, Outperformed, Delivered, Accomplished, Attained
- Generated, Produced, Earned, Secured, Captured, Won
**Improvement:**
- Improved, Enhanced, Optimized, Streamlined, Strengthened, Upgraded, Modernized
- Increased, Boosted, Accelerated, Maximized, Elevated, Amplified
**Creation:**
- Created, Developed, Designed, Built, Established, Founded, Launched
- Pioneered, Initiated, Introduced, Instituted, Originated, Formulated
**Analysis:**
- Analyzed, Evaluated, Assessed, Examined, Investigated, Researched, Audited
- Identified, Diagnosed, Measured, Quantified, Forecasted, Projected
**Communication:**
- Presented, Communicated, Articulated, Negotiated, Persuaded, Influenced
- Collaborated, Partnered, Liaised, Consulted, Advised, Facilitated
**Problem-Solving:**
- Resolved, Solved, Troubleshot, Remedied, Rectified, Corrected, Fixed
- Transformed, Restructured, Reorganized, Revitalized, Turned around
### Quantifying Achievements
**Metrics to Include:**
- **Revenue/Sales**: Dollar amounts, percentage increases
- **Cost Savings**: Money saved, efficiency gains
- **Time**: Hours saved, faster completion, reduced timeline
- **Volume**: Number of units, customers, projects, transactions
- **Percentages**: Growth rates, improvement percentages, error reduction
- **Team Size**: Number of people managed or collaborated with
- **Scope**: Budget size, project scale, geographic reach
- **Quality**: Ratings, satisfaction scores, error rates, accuracy
- **Efficiency**: Productivity improvements, throughput increases
**Before and After Examples:**
**Before (Weak):**
"Managed social media accounts for the company"
**After (Strong):**
"Grew social media following from 5,000 to 50,000+ across 4 platforms in 18 months, increasing engagement rate by 300% and driving 2,000+ website visits monthly"
**Before (Weak):**
"Responsible for customer service"
**After (Strong):**
"Resolved 95% of customer inquiries within 24 hours, improving satisfaction scores from 3.2 to 4.7/5.0 and reducing escalations by 60%"
## 5. Education Section Guidelines
### Standard Format
Degree Name, Major/Field of Study
Institution Name | City, State
Graduation Date: Month Year (or Expected: Month Year)
GPA: X.XX/4.0 (optional - include if 3.5+)
### When to Place Education
**Education BEFORE Experience:**
- Recent graduates (within 2 years)
- Career changers with new relevant degree
- Positions requiring specific degree
- Academic or research positions
- When education is strongest qualification
**Education AFTER Experience:**
- Professionals with 3+ years experience
- When experience is more relevant
- Most mid to senior-level positions
### What to Include
**Always Include:**
- Degree type and major
- Institution name
- Graduation date (month and year)
- Location
**Include When Relevant:**
- GPA (if 3.5+ and recent graduate)
- Honors (cum laude, dean's list, etc.)
- Relevant coursework (for recent grads or career changers)
- Academic projects (if directly relevant)
- Study abroad (if relevant to position)
- Thesis/dissertation title (for advanced degrees)
**Do NOT Include:**
- High school (if you have college degree)
- GPA below 3.5
- Unfinished degrees (unless close to completion)
- Irrelevant coursework
- Standardized test scores (SAT, ACT, GRE) unless required

## 6. Skills Section Guidelines
### Types of Skills Sections
**1. Simple List Format:**
Skills: Python, Java, SQL, AWS, Docker, Kubernetes, Agile, Scrum,
Project Management, Data Analysis, Machine Learning, TensorFlow
**2. Categorized Format (Recommended):**
Technical Skills:
• Programming Languages: Python, Java, JavaScript, C++, SQL
• Frameworks & Libraries: React, Node.js, Django, TensorFlow, Pandas
• Cloud & DevOps: AWS, Azure, Docker, Kubernetes, Jenkins, Git
• Databases: PostgreSQL, MongoDB, Redis, MySQL
Professional Skills:
• Project Management, Agile/Scrum, Team Leadership
• Data Analysis, Business Intelligence, Statistical Modeling
• Technical Documentation, Stakeholder Communication
**3. Proficiency Level Format:**
Technical Proficiencies:
Expert: Python, SQL, AWS, Machine Learning, Data Visualization
Advanced: Java, React, Docker, Kubernetes, Tableau
Intermediate: C++, Azure, TensorFlow, Scala
**4. Tiered Format:**
Core Competencies:
Strategic Planning | Financial Analysis | Team Leadership |
Change Management | Process Improvement | Stakeholder Engagement
Technical Skills:
Excel (Advanced), SQL, Tableau, Power BI, Salesforce, SAP,
QuickBooks, Microsoft Office Suite
### Skill Selection Guidelines
**Include:**
- Skills mentioned in job description
- Industry-standard tools and technologies
- Certifiable skills
- Skills you can demonstrate or discuss
- Both hard skills and relevant soft skills
- Current, in-demand skills
**Exclude:**
- Outdated technologies (unless specifically required)
- Basic skills (Microsoft Word for professionals)
- Skills you learned but never used professionally
- Overly generic terms ("hardworking," "team player")
- Skills you can't discuss in interview
- Irrelevant hobbies or personal interests
### Hard Skills vs. Soft Skills
**Hard Skills (Technical/Teachable):**
- Programming languages
- Software applications
- Technical methodologies
- Industry-specific tools
- Foreign languages
- Data analysis
- Certifications
**Soft Skills (Interpersonal/Behavioral):**
- Leadership
- Communication
- Problem-solving
- Critical thinking
- Adaptability
- Collaboration
- Time management
**Best Practice:** Focus primarily on hard skills in Skills section; demonstrate soft skills through achievements in Experience section.
### Skills Section Mistakes to Avoid
**1. Skill Inflation:**
❌ "Expert in Python" (after 3-month bootcamp)
✅ "Proficient in Python with 2 years of project experience"
**2. Vague Terms:**
❌ "Good with computers"
✅ "Windows Server Administration, Active Directory, PowerShell"
**3. Outdated Skills:**
❌ "MS-DOS, Windows 95, Lotus Notes"
✅ Current versions and relevant technologies
**4. Over-categorization:**
❌ 10+ skill categories with 2 items each
✅ 3-5 meaningful categories with relevant skills
**5. Soft Skills Only:**
❌ "Hardworking, Dedicated, Team Player, Fast Learner"
✅ Specific technical skills with soft skills demonstrated in experience
## 7. Formatting and Design Guidelines
### Font Selection
**Recommended Professional Fonts:**
- **Sans-Serif**: Arial, Calibri, Helvetica, Trebuchet MS, Verdana
- **Serif**: Georgia, Times New Roman, Garamond, Cambria
- **Modern**: Lato, Open Sans, Roboto (if using PDF)
**Font Sizes:**
- Name/Header: 18-24pt
- Section Headers: 12-14pt
- Body Text: 10-12pt
- Minimum: 10pt (nothing smaller)
**Font Guidelines:**
- Use maximum 2 fonts (one for headers, one for body)
- Maintain consistency throughout
- Ensure readability
- Avoid decorative or script fonts
### Margins and Spacing
**Margins:**
- Standard: 0.5" to 1" on all sides
- Minimum: 0.5"
- Maximum: 1"
- Adjust to fit content naturally
**Line Spacing:**
- Body text: 1.0 to 1.15
- Between sections: 1.5 to 2.0
- After bullets: Single space
- Between job entries: 1.5 line spacing
**White Space:**
- Essential for readability
- Don't cram too much content
- Balance text with empty space
- Use spacing to create visual hierarchy
### Color Guidelines
**Safe Color Schemes:**
- Black text on white background (safest)
- Dark gray (#333333) on white
- Navy blue headers (#000080) with black text
- Minimal accent color for headers only
**Colors to Avoid:**
- Bright colors (red, yellow, orange)
- Light text on dark backgrounds
- Multiple colors throughout
- Colored backgrounds
- Any color that doesn't print well in black and white
**Best Practice:** Stick to black and white or add one conservative accent color for headers only.
### Bullet Points and Lists
**Bullet Style:**
- Simple round bullets (•)
- Hyphens (-)
- Small squares (■)
- Keep consistent throughout
**Bullet Point Rules:**
- Start with action verb
- Keep parallel structure
- One idea per bullet
- 1-2 lines maximum per bullet
- 3-8 bullets per position
### Visual Hierarchy
**Create Clear Hierarchy:**
1. **Name** (Largest, 18-24pt)
2. **Section Headers** (Bold, 12-14pt)
3. **Job Titles** (Bold, 11-12pt)
4. **Company Names** (11-12pt, may be bold or regular)
5. **Body Text** (10-12pt)
6. **Dates/Locations** (10-11pt, may be italicized)
## 8. Content Quality Standards
### Writing Style
**Use:**
- Active voice ("Led team" not "Team was led by me")
- Past tense for previous roles
- Present tense for current role
- Concise, direct language
- Industry-specific terminology
- Quantifiable metrics
- Specific examples
**Avoid:**
- First person pronouns (I, me, my, we)
- Third person (he, she, they)
- Complete sentences (use phrases)
- Passive voice
- Jargon or acronyms without context
- Clichés and buzzwords
- Negative language
### Grammar and Punctuation
**Consistency Rules:**
- Use periods at end of bullets consistently (all or none)
- Maintain parallel structure in lists
- Keep verb tense consistent within sections
- Use same date format throughout
- Standardize abbreviations
**Common Errors to Avoid:**
- Spelling mistakes (use spell-check)
- Wrong verb tense
- Inconsistent formatting
- Missing periods or punctuation
- Incorrect use of apostrophes
- Run-on sentences
### Honesty and Accuracy
**Always Be Truthful:**
- Never lie about degrees or certifications
- Don't exaggerate job titles
- Be accurate with dates
- Don't claim others' achievements
- Verify all information is current
**Ethical Guidelines:**
- You can frame information positively
- You can emphasize relevant experience
- You can omit irrelevant information
- You cannot fabricate information
- You cannot misrepresent facts

## 10. Common Mistakes to Avoid
### Content Mistakes
1. **Typos and Grammatical Errors**
   - Always proofread multiple times
   - Use spell-check
   - Have someone else review
   - Read backwards to catch errors
2. **Including Irrelevant Information**
   - Unrelated work experience
   - Personal hobbies (unless relevant)
   - Outdated skills
   - High school information (if you have college degree)
3. **Being Too Vague**
   - "Responsible for sales" vs "Increased sales by 35%"
   - "Managed team" vs "Led team of 12 engineers"
   - "Worked on projects" vs "Delivered 5 projects on time and under budget"
4. **Listing Duties Instead of Achievements**
   - Focus on what you accomplished, not just what you did
   - Use metrics and results
   - Show impact and value
5. **Using Unprofessional Email**
   - partygirl@email.com ❌
   - firstname.lastname@email.com ✅
### Formatting Mistakes
1. **Inconsistent Formatting**
   - Mixed fonts
   - Varying bullet styles
   - Inconsistent date formats
   - Different spacing
2. **Poor Visual Hierarchy**
   - Everything same size
   - No clear sections
   - Cluttered appearance
   - Hard to scan quickly
3. **Too Much or Too Little White Space**
   - Cramming too much content
   - Excessive empty space
   - Unbalanced layout
4. **Using Templates Incorrectly**
   - Not customizing template
   - Leaving placeholder text
   - Template doesn't fit content
   - Overly decorative templates
### Strategic Mistakes
1. **One-Size-Fits-All Resume**
   - Not tailoring to job description
   - Generic professional summary
   - Missing key required skills
   - No keyword optimization
2. **Focusing on Responsibilities Instead of Results**
   - What you were supposed to do vs what you achieved
   - Process vs outcomes
   - Activities vs impact
3. **Including Salary Information**
   - Never include current/desired salary
   - Don't include salary history
   - Discuss in interview, not on resume
4. **Unexplained Employment Gaps**
   - Address gaps honestly
   - Show what you did during gap
   - Use years only if helpful (2020-2022 vs Jan 2020-Mar 2022)
5. **Too Long or Too Short**
   - 5-page resume for mid-level role
   - One page for 20 years experience
   - Including every job ever held

## 12. File Naming and Submission
### File Naming Best Practices
**Recommended Format:**
FirstName_LastName_Resume.pdf
FirstName_LastName_Resume.docx
John_Smith_Resume.pdf
Jane_Doe_Resume_2024.pdf
**Include Date if Multiple Versions:**
John_Smith_Resume_Jan2024.pdf
Jane_Doe_Resume_TechRole_2024.pdf
**Avoid:**
Resume.pdf (too generic)
MyResume_Final_Final_v3.docx (unprofessional)
JohnResume2024NEW.pdf (poor formatting)
resume (1).pdf (default download name)
### File Format Selection
**When to Use .docx:**
- ATS requires Word format
- Employer specifically requests .doc or .docx
- Applying through system that specifies Word
- Conservative industry (finance, law, government)
**When to Use .pdf:**
- Employer accepts PDF
- Want to preserve exact formatting
- Using non-standard fonts or design
- Sending via email
- Creative industries
- Most modern applications
**General Rule:** PDF is usually safer for preserving formatting, but always check job posting requirements.
## 13. Proofreading and Quality Control
### Proofreading Checklist
**Content Review:**
- [ ] All dates are accurate
- [ ] Company names spelled correctly
- [ ] Job titles are accurate
- [ ] No spelling errors
- [ ] Grammar is correct
- [ ] Verb tenses consistent
- [ ] All bullet points have parallel structure
- [ ] Contact information is current
- [ ] No personal pronouns (I, me, my)
- [ ] All achievements quantified where possible
**Formatting Review:**
- [ ] Consistent font throughout
- [ ] Consistent bullet style
- [ ] Proper spacing between sections
- [ ] Margins are appropriate
- [ ] Page breaks in logical places (if 2+ pages)
- [ ] No orphaned headers at bottom of page
- [ ] Alignment is consistent
- [ ] No extra spaces or formatting issues
**ATS Compatibility:**
- [ ] No text boxes or tables
- [ ] No headers/footers with critical info
- [ ] Standard section headers
- [ ] Simple formatting
- [ ] Keywords from job description included
- [ ] File format appropriate (.docx or PDF)
**Strategic Review:**
- [ ] Tailored to specific job/industry
- [ ] Most relevant information prominent
- [ ] Professional summary compelling
- [ ] Achievements over responsibilities
- [ ] Keywords naturally integrated
- [ ] Length appropriate for experience level
### Proofreading Techniques
1. **Read Backwards** - Catches spelling errors
2. **Read Aloud** - Catches awkward phrasing
3. **Print It Out** - See errors you miss on screen
4. **Take a Break** - Review with fresh eyes
5. **Use Tools** - Grammarly, spell-check, etc.
6. **Get Second Opinion** - Have someone else review
7. **Check on Different Devices** - Ensure formatting holds
### Common Errors to Check
**Spelling:**
- Company names
- Industry-specific terms
- Technical terminology
- Proper nouns
- Homophones (their/there, your/you're)
**Grammar:**
- Subject-verb agreement
- Parallel structure in lists
- Consistent verb tense
- Proper punctuation
- Comma usage
**Formatting:**
- Inconsistent spacing
- Mixed fonts
- Varying bullet styles
- Alignment issues
- Page break problems
## 14. Resume Maintenance
### When to Update
**Update Immediately After:**
- Earning a new certification or completing a course
- Receiving a promotion or title change
- Completing a major project with measurable results
- Acquiring a significant new skill or tool
- Leaving a position (add end date and final achievements)
- Winning an award, recognition, or publication

**Update on a Regular Schedule:**
- At minimum every 6 months, even if not job searching
- After your annual performance review (incorporates achievements)
- At the start of each new year (refresh dates and remove stale content)
- Before attending any networking event or industry conference

**Update When Job Searching:**
- Before every application — tailor to that specific role
- After each interview (incorporate feedback and gaps identified)
- After adding a new reference or professional connection

### Version Management
**Master Resume:**
- Keep one comprehensive master file with all experience, skills, certifications, and achievements
- Do not send the master resume directly — always tailor a copy
- Store in a secure, backed-up location (cloud storage recommended)
- Label clearly: `FirstName_LastName_Resume_MASTER.docx`

**Tailored Versions:**
- Create a new copy from master for each application
- Name with role and date: `FirstName_LastName_Resume_SoftwareEngineer_2024.docx`
- Keep both .docx and PDF versions of each submission copy
- Archive submissions — useful if you are asked back for a second interview

**Tracking Submissions:**
- Maintain a simple log (spreadsheet) with: Company, Role, Date Sent, Version Used, Status
- Makes follow-up and interview prep significantly easier
- Helps you identify which resume versions perform best

### Keeping Content Fresh
- Remove experience older than 15 years unless highly relevant
- Drop obsolete technologies and skills as they age out
- Replace weak bullet points with stronger ones as you gain new achievements
- Review your professional summary every 6 months for relevance and accuracy
- Ensure LinkedIn profile stays in sync with the resume
