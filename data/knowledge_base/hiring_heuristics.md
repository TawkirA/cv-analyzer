# Hiring Heuristics - Recruiter Decision-Making Patterns
## Overview
Understanding how recruiters and hiring managers evaluate resumes, make decisions, and what triggers positive or negative responses during the screening process.
## 1. The 6-Second Rule
### Initial Resume Scan
**What Recruiters Look For in 6 Seconds:**
First Glance (2 seconds):
✓ Name and contact information
✓ Current/most recent job title
✓ Current/most recent company
✓ Overall visual appearance
Quick Scan (4 seconds):
✓ Previous job titles and companies
✓ Education credentials
✓ Employment dates and tenure
✓ Key skills section
✓ Overall career progression
Decision Point:
After 6 seconds, recruiter decides:
Continue reading (detailed review)
Reject (move to next resume)
Maybe pile (review later if time)
**What Stands Out Positively:**
✓ Recognizable company names
✓ Impressive job titles matching the role
✓ Clear career progression
✓ Relevant education/certifications
✓ Clean, professional formatting
✓ Quantified achievements (numbers catch the eye)
✓ Keywords matching job description
✓ Appropriate length (1-2 pages)
**What Stands Out Negatively:**
❌ Typos or errors (immediate red flag)
❌ Unprofessional email address
❌ Confusing or cluttered layout
❌ Irrelevant experience
❌ Employment gaps without explanation
❌ Job hopping (many short stints)
❌ Outdated or missing information
❌ Too long (3+ pages for non-executive)
### Making It Past the 6-Second Test
**Optimization Strategy:**
Top Third of Page (Most Critical):
Name - Bold, prominent, professional
Contact Info - Complete, professional, easy to find
Professional Summary - Compelling, keyword-rich, relevant
Most Recent Position - Title, company, dates visible
Visual Hierarchy:
Important information stands out
Easy to scan quickly
Clear section divisions
Strategic use of bold/formatting
White space for readability
Content Priority:
Most relevant information first
Keywords prominent
Achievements over duties
Quantified results visible
Clear value proposition
## 2. Recruiter Psychology
### Cognitive Biases in Resume Screening
**Halo Effect:**
Definition: One positive trait influences overall perception
Examples:
✓ Prestigious university → Assume competent
✓ Well-known company → Assume qualified
✓ Professional appearance → Assume attention to detail
✓ Impressive first achievement → Assume all are impressive
How to Leverage:
Lead with strongest credentials
Mention recognizable brands/schools
Start bullets with impressive results
Professional formatting throughout
**Recency Bias:**
Definition: Most recent information has greatest impact
Examples:
Most recent job weighted heavily
Recent achievements more memorable
Current skills valued over old ones
Latest education/certifications noticed
How to Leverage:
Emphasize current role achievements
List recent certifications first
Update skills section regularly
Highlight recent relevant projects
Detailed bullets for recent positions
**Confirmation Bias:**
Definition: Seeking information that confirms initial impression
Examples:
Good first impression → Looking for supporting evidence
Bad first impression → Looking for reasons to reject
Keyword match → Seeking more matches
Red flag → Looking for more problems
How to Leverage:
Create strong positive first impression
Consistent quality throughout
No red flags in visible areas
Reinforce qualifications repeatedly
**Pattern Recognition:**
Definition: Recruiters look for familiar success patterns
What They Recognize:
✓ Similar career paths to successful hires
✓ Familiar company names in their industry
✓ Expected progression (junior → senior)
✓ Standard educational backgrounds
✓ Common certification paths
✓ Typical skill combinations
How to Leverage:
Research successful people in target role
Mirror career progression patterns
Include industry-standard credentials
Use familiar terminology
Show logical career development
### Decision-Making Shortcuts
**The "Gut Feeling" Factor:**
What Influences It:
Overall professional appearance
Clarity and organization
Confidence in presentation
Attention to detail
Cultural fit indicators
Communication style in writing
Positive Signals:
✓ Polished, professional presentation
✓ Clear communication
✓ Confident but not arrogant tone
✓ Attention to detail evident
✓ Appropriate formality level
✓ Well-organized information
Negative Signals:
❌ Sloppy formatting
❌ Unclear or confusing content
❌ Overly casual or overly formal
❌ Inconsistencies
❌ Missing information
❌ Poor grammar/spelling
**The Comparison Game:**
Recruiters Compare:
You vs. job requirements (60% match minimum)
You vs. other candidates
You vs. current employees in similar roles
You vs. ideal candidate profile
You vs. previous successful hires
Standing Out:
✓ Exceed minimum qualifications
✓ Unique combination of skills
✓ Impressive quantified achievements
✓ Relevant specialized experience
✓ Additional valuable certifications
✓ Culture fit indicators
## 3. Red Flags That Trigger Rejection
### Critical Red Flags (Immediate Rejection)
**1. Typos and Errors:**
Impact: 58% of resumes rejected for typos
Why: Indicates lack of attention to detail
Examples:
❌ "Managed team of 10 employes"
❌ "Experiance in project management"
❌ Wrong company names
❌ Incorrect dates
Prevention:
✓ Proofread multiple times
✓ Use spell-check
✓ Have someone else review
✓ Read backwards
✓ Print and review on paper
**2. Unprofessional Email:**
Impact: Immediate negative impression
Examples:
❌ partygirl123@email.com
❌ hottstuff@email.com
❌ ihatework@email.com
Fix:
✓ firstname.lastname@email.com
✓ Create professional email if needed
✓ Use Gmail or Outlook
**3. Obvious Lies:**
Impact: Automatic disqualification if caught
Common Lies:
❌ Fake degrees
❌ Inflated job titles
❌ Extended employment dates
❌ Claiming others' achievements
❌ False certifications
Reality:
Background checks catch these
References will reveal truth
Skills tests expose false claims
Industry is small, people talk
**4. Inappropriate Content:**
Red Flags:
❌ Photo (in US resumes)
❌ Personal information (age, marital status)
❌ Political/religious affiliations
❌ Controversial interests
❌ Salary demands on resume
❌ Reasons for leaving jobs
❌ Negative comments about employers
### Warning Flags (Require Explanation)
**Employment Gaps:**
Concern: What were you doing?
Acceptable Explanations:
✓ Family caregiving
✓ Health issues (brief mention)
✓ Further education
✓ Sabbatical/travel
✓ Freelance/consulting
✓ Layoff/company closure
How to Address:
Be honest but brief
Show productive activity
Demonstrate current skills
Express readiness to work
Don't over-explain
**Job Hopping:**
Concern: Will you stay?
Red Flag Pattern:
❌ Multiple jobs under 1 year
❌ No clear progression
❌ Lateral moves only
❌ 5+ jobs in 5 years
Acceptable Explanations:
✓ Contract positions (labeled)
✓ Company layoffs/closures
✓ Career advancement (promotions)
✓ Industry standard (consulting)
✓ Relocation
How to Address:
Consolidate contract work
Note circumstances (layoffs, etc.)
Show recent stability
Emphasize commitment in cover letter
**Lack of Career Progression:**
Concern: Why no advancement?
Red Flags:
❌ Same title for 10+ years
❌ No increasing responsibility
❌ No skill development
❌ Lateral moves only
Mitigation:
✓ Show expanded responsibilities
✓ Highlight special projects
✓ Emphasize skill development
✓ Note industry-specific factors
✓ Demonstrate continuous learning
**Overqualification:**
Concern: Will you leave quickly?
When It's a Problem:
PhD applying for entry-level
C-level applying for manager role
20 years experience for junior position
Salary expectations too high
How to Address:
Explain genuine interest
Show long-term commitment
Emphasize cultural fit
Demonstrate realistic expectations
Address in cover letter

## 6. Decision-Making Timeline
### ATS Screening Phase (Seconds)
**Automated Filtering:**
Time: Instant to 24 hours
Process:
Resume uploaded to ATS
System parses content
Extracts key information
Matches against job requirements
Calculates match score
Ranks candidates
Pass Rate: 25-30% typically advance
What Determines Pass/Fail:
✓ Keyword match percentage (60%+ needed)
✓ Required qualifications present
✓ Years of experience match
✓ Education requirements met
✓ Location match (if relevant)
✓ Parsing success (no errors)
Optimization:
ATS-friendly formatting
All required keywords included
Clear section headers
Standard fonts
No tables or text boxes
Text-based PDF or .docx
### Human Recruiter Review (6-30 Seconds)
**Initial Screening:**
Time: 6 seconds average, up to 30 seconds if interested
Process:
Quick visual scan
Check key qualifications
Assess overall fit
Make initial decision
Pass Rate: 10-20% advance to detailed review
What They Look For:
✓ Relevant job titles
✓ Required skills present
✓ Appropriate experience level
✓ Education credentials
✓ Professional appearance
✓ No obvious red flags
Decision Outcomes:
Yes (detailed review): 15%
No (reject): 70%
Maybe (review if time): 15%
### Detailed Review (2-5 Minutes)
**Deep Dive Assessment:**
Time: 2-5 minutes for promising candidates
Process:
Read professional summary
Review work experience in detail
Assess achievements and impact
Check education and certifications
Evaluate skills match
Consider cultural fit indicators
Note questions for interview
Pass Rate: 50-70% advance to phone screen
What They Evaluate:
✓ Depth of relevant experience
✓ Quality of achievements
✓ Quantified results
✓ Career trajectory
✓ Stability and tenure
✓ Communication skills (writing)
✓ Attention to detail
✓ Overall fit with role
Decision Criteria:
Strong match: Move to phone screen
Good match with questions: Schedule call
Marginal: Hold for comparison
Not quite right: Reject with feedback
### Hiring Manager Review (5-15 Minutes)
**Final Resume Assessment:**
Time: 5-15 minutes before interview
Process:
Thorough review of resume
Compare to role requirements
Identify strengths and gaps
Prepare interview questions
Research candidate background
Review LinkedIn profile
Check for mutual connections
What They Focus On:
✓ Technical competency
✓ Relevant experience depth
✓ Problem-solving examples
✓ Leadership potential
✓ Team fit
✓ Growth trajectory
✓ Specific accomplishments
Interview Preparation:

Red flags to address

## 9. Unconscious Bias Awareness
### Common Biases in Hiring
**Name Bias:**
Reality:
Resumes with "white-sounding" names get 50% more callbacks
Gender-neutral names may face uncertainty
Foreign names may face discrimination
Mitigation Strategies:
✓ Focus on qualifications
✓ Strong professional summary
✓ Quantified achievements
✓ Recognizable credentials
✓ Professional presentation
✓ LinkedIn profile consistency
Note: Some candidates use initials or anglicized names,
though this shouldn't be necessary.
**Education Bias:**
Reality:
Ivy League/top schools get preference
State schools may be overlooked
Non-traditional education (bootcamps) scrutinized
Overcoming:
✓ Emphasize results over pedigree
✓ Highlight relevant coursework
✓ Include certifications
✓ Demonstrate applied skills
✓ Show continuous learning
✓ Quantify achievements
**Employment Gap Bias:**
Reality:
Gaps often viewed negatively
Assumptions made without context
Women penalized more than men
Addressing:
✓ Explain briefly and honestly
✓ Show productive activities
✓ Emphasize current readiness
✓ Demonstrate maintained skills
✓ Don't over-apologize
**Age Bias:**
Younger Candidates:
Concern: Lack of experience
Mitigation: Emphasize achievements, maturity, quick learning
Older Candidates:
Concern: Overqualified, outdated skills, expensive
Mitigation: Current skills, flexibility, relevant experience only
Strategies:
✓ Omit graduation dates (if 15+ years ago)
✓ Focus on recent experience (10-15 years)
✓ Highlight current technology skills
✓ Show adaptability and learning
✓ Demonstrate value, not years
